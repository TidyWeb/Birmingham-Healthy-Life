Chapter 11

How I worked

What was hard, what I changed my mind about, and how I worked with AI along the way.

Letting go of the railway

The railway idea is what got me started, and I held on to it for too long. It was a good story, and I'd already gathered the station data. But I realised it was shaping the question to fit the picture. Once I saw that the interesting thing was neighbours, and not stations, the railway had to go. I've kept the story of where the idea came from, and nothing else.

Tableau, and building my own

I built the Tableau dashboard first, because the course asks for a business intelligence tool. It worked, but I found it shallow, and I spent more time fighting the editor than thinking about the data. Building my own dashboard took longer. But it let me decide every detail, like how a picked area is shown, which way every axis runs, and what the colours mean.

Data that doesn't line up

Almost nothing came at the right size. Fuel poverty came for smaller areas, pollution came as a grid, crime came as individual records, and the years run from 2019 to 2024. Getting all of it onto the same 660 areas, without quietly bending any of it, was most of the work in Notebook 02.

Bugs that only showed up somewhere else

My notebooks ran perfectly on my machine, and then broke in a fresh copy of the repository. The culprits were folder names, capital letters and paths that only existed on my computer. So I learned to test from a clean copy before I publish anything.

Distrusting a result that looked too good

My Census screen confirmed 81 characteristics. That felt like a lot, and it was. When I combined them, I found most of them were one picture told 81 ways. I'd rather report one honest finding than 81 impressive ones.

Working with AI

I used AI throughout this project: Gemini, Codex and Claude, at every stage.

It wasn't peripheral, it is fundamental to this whole project.

I used Claude as my idea sounding board. I wanted to explore something meaningful to me, and used Claude to bounce ideas off. Some stuck.

I used Google Gemini as my research assistant. We would seek out open and available datasets together, examine them for suitability, and reject the vast majority as unfit.

Codex was my coding partner. I would shape out rough code ideas in VS Code, outline plans and steps, then call on my handbuilt coding Robot Kevin who exists as a skill and as an entity with memory to everything I have learned from the LMS on this course. Kevin still needs a firm hand, as he can get carried away without close supervision and guidance.

So AI in the form of LLMs helped me find out what data was out there, talk through ideas, write code and draft wording. But I decided what to look at, which sources to use, what to leave out, and correct when a result didn't add up, or added up suspiciously too well. The majority of text is mine, and it says what I mean, and every number on this site comes from a notebook's output. My first notebook has a section on how I worked.

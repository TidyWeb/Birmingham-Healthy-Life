Chapter 10

What I learned from the data

What my six notebooks add up to, and where it stops.

What I found

The gaps between neighbours are real. 69.8% of neighbour pairs differ by more than estimate noise can explain, where pure chance would give about 5%. The biggest gap, between New Oscott and Perry Common College Road, is 15.85 years. Chapter 5

Income, and what travels with it, accounts for most of the difference between areas. My seven measures predict HLE to within about a year and a half in councils the model never saw, and income carries by far the most weight. Chapter 8

It isn't just richer councils sitting next to poorer ones. When I take each council's average off, income still carries the most weight, and my measures still account for 86.6% of what's left. Chapter 9

The sharpest drops sit on boundaries. Two-thirds of the hotspot boundaries also divide councils, against a quarter of ordinary neighbours. But one hotspot is entirely inside Birmingham. Chapter 9

What my measures miss looks more like settledness, or wealth, than income. Areas of settled, owner-occupied households do better than I'd predict, and areas of single people renting do worse. Chapter 9

Some places don't fit at all. Castle Bromwich East does nearly 5 years better than its measures suggest, and Central Solihull does 7 years better. Frankley, Druids Heath and parts of south Birmingham do worse. Those are the places I'd look at next. Chapter 8

The limits

  • Areas, not people. Every number I give describes an area. None of it says anything about the health of a person who lives there.
  • Links, not causes. Income lines up with HLE, but that doesn't show income causes it. And my Census screen can't say why owning matters.
  • Different years. HLE covers 2019 to 2023. The Census was in 2021, and my pollution, fuel poverty and crime figures are from 2024. I'm assuming the order of the areas held, and I haven't tested that.
  • Estimates. HLE is an estimate with its own margin of error, and my average of men and women is my own measure. Its margin of error is probably a little too narrow.
  • Chosen from HLE itself. I picked the big-gap pairs and the hotspots because their gaps are extreme, so some of what I found about them is built in. And I chose the Census additions from the same misses they fix.
  • Neighbours aren't independent. Areas next to each other share a lot, and that makes some of my statistics more confident than they should be. Splitting the data by council is my main guard against that.
